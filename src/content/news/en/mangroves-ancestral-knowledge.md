Faced with the failure of mangrove protection laws, some countries and NGOs are turning to endogenous cults and ancestral knowledge.

## Why mangroves matter

Mangroves are a natural barrier against erosion and a powerful tool in the fight against climate change, because of their high carbon sequestration capacity — often several times that of terrestrial forest per hectare. They are also nurseries: a large share of coastal fisheries depends on them at some stage of the life cycle.

## When the law is not enough

Legal protection assumes enforcement, and enforcement assumes presence. On long, remote coastlines with limited state capacity, a decree changes little on its own.

What has proven more durable in several places is protection rooted in local belief and social sanction. Where a stand of mangrove is regarded as sacred, cutting it carries a cost that no fine has managed to impose.

## A question worth sitting with

This raises an uncomfortable but productive question for conservation practice: whose authority actually protects an ecosystem? The answer, on this coast, is often not the one written into law.
