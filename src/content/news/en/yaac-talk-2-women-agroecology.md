On **1 November 2025**, the Youth Alliance for Agroecology and Climate held its second YAAC Talk, dedicated to the theme *“Women, knowledge and agroecology: at the heart of sustainable climate adaptation.”*

For more than two hours and thirty minutes, women leaders, changemakers and environmental advocates came together to share their experiences, their obstacles and their vision.

## Knowledge that is already there

The session pushed back against a common assumption in climate programming: that adaptation techniques must be imported. Across the continent, women farmers already hold detailed knowledge of seed selection, soil cover, water management and crop association — knowledge built over generations and refined season by season.

What is missing is not expertise. It is recognition, land rights and access to finance.

## Recurring themes

- **Seed sovereignty.** Control over local varieties is control over the ability to adapt.
- **Time poverty.** Adaptation work is unpaid work, and it lands disproportionately on women.
- **Transmission.** Knowledge that is not documented and taught is one generation away from disappearing.

The conversation ended on a concrete commitment: to document these practices and feed them into YAAC’s training programmes.
